Beginner Guide

Build a Password You'll Actually Remember

"Use a strong password" is advice everyone gives and almost nobody follows well, because most strong-password advice produces passwords no human can remember. There's a better way — and it doesn't involve a random string of symbols.

Forget the old rules

The classic advice — one capital letter, one number, one symbol, eight characters — actually produces weak, guessable passwords like Password1!. Length matters far more than complexity.

Use a passphrase instead

String together four or five random, unrelated words: correct-horse-battery-staple style. A 20-character passphrase like this is dramatically harder to crack than an 8-character mix of symbols, and much easier to remember.

Why it works: password-cracking tools guess based on length and pattern. More characters means exponentially more possible combinations, even with "simple" words.

Never reuse passwords across sites

This is the single biggest risk. If one site gets breached — and breaches happen constantly — attackers try that same email/password combination on every other major site. Reusing passwords turns one breach into dozens of compromised accounts.

Use a password manager

You don't need to memorize a unique passphrase for every single account. A password manager (built into your browser or phone, or a dedicated app) generates and stores strong, unique passwords for everything — you only need to remember one master password.

Quick checklist

  • At least 16 characters, or a 4-5 word passphrase.
  • Unique for every single account — no exceptions for "unimportant" ones.
  • Stored in a password manager, not a notes app or sticky note.
  • Paired with two-factor authentication wherever it's offered.

A long, unique passphrase you actually remember beats a "strong" password you have to reset every month because you forgot it.
