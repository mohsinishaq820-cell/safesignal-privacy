Lock Down Your Social Media

Most people's social media privacy settings are still on whatever the platform defaulted to when they signed up — and defaults are almost always set to share more, not less. Here's what to actually check.

Audit who can see your posts

On every platform, there's a setting for post visibility — public, friends, or custom. If it's set to public and you didn't choose that deliberately, anyone, including strangers and future employers, can see your history.

Review your tagged photos and location data

Photos taken on a smartphone often carry embedded location data. Many platforms strip this automatically on upload, but not all do — and tagged photos can reveal patterns about where you live, work, or travel.

Quick fix: turn off "precise location" for social apps in your phone's settings, and review tagging permissions so you approve tags before they appear on your profile.

Check what apps are still connected

Every quiz, game, or app you ever linked to a social account may still have access to your data, sometimes years later. Go to your account's "Apps and Websites" or "Connected Apps" settings and remove anything you don't recognize or no longer use.

Limit what's visible to "everyone"

Birthday, phone number, email, hometown, workplace — these are often visible by default. Individually they seem harmless, but combined they're enough for identity theft or a convincing phishing attempt.

A 10-minute privacy pass

  1. Set future posts to friends-only (or your preferred limited audience).
  2. Review and restrict who can see your friends/followers list.
  3. Remove unused connected apps.
  4. Hide your birthday, phone number, and email from public view.
  5. Turn off precise location sharing.

None of this means disappearing from social media — it means deciding, deliberately, who gets to see what.
